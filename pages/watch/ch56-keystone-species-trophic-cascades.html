Why Watch This?

This film follows Robert Paine’s sea-star removal experiment and later sea otter research to show how a species can have effects far beyond what its abundance suggests. It is the primary Week 7 resource for top-down control, keystone species, indirect effects, and trophic cascades.

What to Watch For

Bottom-up versus top-down regulation: identify what initiates change in each model.

Keystone species: explain why ecological importance is not determined simply by abundance.

Paine’s experiment: connect removal of Pisaster ochraceus to mussel expansion and declining species richness.

Sea otter cascade: trace the sequence from otters to sea urchins to kelp.

Indirect effects: notice how a predator can affect organisms several trophic levels away.

Scientific evidence: distinguish direct field manipulation from comparisons, long-term observations, and historical inference.

Corrections and Clarifications

Name and organism: the ecologist is Robert Paine, and the ochre sea star is Pisaster ochraceus.

Green World Hypothesis: urchin-dominated pools do not show that herbivores are always controlled. They show what can happen when a higher predator is absent and herbivores suppress producers.

Whaling connection: the proposed chain from commercial whaling to orca predation on sea otters is supported by ecological evidence, but it is more inferential than Paine’s direct removal experiment.
