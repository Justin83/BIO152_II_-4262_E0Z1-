What to Watch For

Niches and competition: distinguish fundamental from realized niches and connect overlap with competitive exclusion.

Resource partitioning: explain how coexisting species reduce competition.

Predation and defenses: connect consumer pressure with adaptations such as camouflage, warning coloration, and mimicry.

Symbiosis: distinguish mutualism, commensalism, and parasitism.

Succession and disturbance: compare primary and secondary succession without assuming every community follows one fixed endpoint.

Corrections and Clarifications

Scientific names: corrected caption spellings include Chthamalus, Semibalanus, Geospiza fuliginosa, Taenia solium, and Dicrocoelium dendriticum.

Coral snakes: they are venomous, not poisonous.

Spanish moss: mature plants lack functional roots and absorb water and nutrients primarily through specialized leaf scales.

Poison-dart frogs: many defensive alkaloids are acquired from dietary arthropods.

Paine’s experiment: Pisaster ochraceus primarily controlled mussels, especially Mytilus; describing the result chiefly as control of barnacles is misleading.

Succession and disturbance: succession does not always follow one fixed path, and the intermediate disturbance hypothesis is context-dependent rather than universal.
