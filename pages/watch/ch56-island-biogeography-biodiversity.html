Why Watch This?

This film begins with E. O. Wilson’s island observations and experimental work on mangrove islands, then applies island-biogeography principles to habitat fragments and wildlife corridors. It is the primary Week 7 resource for species–area relationships, colonization, extinction, and connectivity.

What to Watch For

Species–area relationship: larger areas generally support more species.

Colonization and extinction: explain how these opposing processes influence the number of species on an island.

Area and isolation: smaller area generally increases extinction risk, while greater isolation generally reduces immigration.

Species turnover: a community may return to a similar number of species without regaining the same species.

Habitat fragments as islands: connect roads, farms, and development with isolation of habitat patches.

Wildlife corridors: explain how connectivity can promote dispersal and reduce functional isolation.

Corrections and Clarifications

Daniel Simberloff: automated captions may misspell his surname.

Mangrove experiment: the defaunation work targeted terrestrial arthropods; it did not literally remove every form of life.

Krakatoa: describing the post-eruption islands as completely lifeless is a useful narrative simplification, not a claim that every microhabitat was sterilized.

Equilibrium: similar species richness after recolonization does not mean the original community returned; species identity can change through turnover.
