Why Watch This?

This film follows decades of Serengeti research to show how removal of rinderpest allowed wildebeest to increase—and how that population change altered grass, fire, trees, birds, and mammals. It is the featured Chapter 55 case study for keystone species and indirect community effects.

What to Watch For

Keystone species: explain why wildebeest affect the community far beyond their numerical abundance.

Indirect effects: trace the sequence from wildebeest grazing to reduced fire, greater tree recruitment, and changes in other animals.

Bottom-up and top-down control: identify the evidence used to test whether food or predators regulate herbivores.

Scientific evidence: notice aerial counts, carcass and marrow analysis, GPS tracking, satellite vegetation data, and long-term photographs.

Migration: connect seasonal movement with food availability and predator exposure.

Corrections and Clarifications

Population versus community ecology: the first portion focuses heavily on Chapter 54 concepts. That evidence is necessary to understand the Chapter 55 cascade beginning around 29:00.

Resident versus migratory mortality: the supplied captions contain an internally inconsistent percentage comparison. Focus on the supported conclusion that migration changes exposure to food limitation and predators.

Correlation and mechanism: long-term correlations are strengthened by the demonstrated mechanism: wildebeest consumed grass, leaving less dry fuel for fires.
